<br>
Stopped by the thrift store yesterday. 
With all the obvious glaring back at me — there stood dilapidated hand-me-downs, and leftovers. 
Chaos that consumed the warehouse.

&nbsp;There was no rhyme or reason to go, I have plenty of clothes. Not looking to revolutionize an already nonexistent style in me, but...that cheap dopamine hit when an orphaned piece of cloth fits ever so perfectly? 
When there's no question that it was made for you, then discarded, all for your rescue? It's a nondestructive high, certainly better than my other ones — I'll happily chase these.

&nbsp;I wandered through the endless clutter for a while, and once the walls bagan circling in...the clothes began to fill out. 
I see myself in everything.
Imagining the figure of who held these up...was this fitted on them? 
Or loose like a nightgown? 

Who adorned it all...
man? 
Woman?

Were they defying something primal and going to a section they've never been allowed to before? I wonder...I don't buy anything, nor try anything on. 
There was a cosmic tug, taunting me towards the appliances...and I remembered that $5 air-fryer I got here months ago —

"Maybe I can get that good of a deal this time."

Between my sifting, I remember all the frozen foods that were donated to me,

"Does that stuff ever go bad?" 
"No one ever explains these things to me."
<br>
&nbsp;Shifting myself adjacent to the used cooking attire, I find what seems to be, too well organized family memorabilia...notebooks, yearbooks, scrapbooks, all used and filled to the brim, of course. Maybe it's all sat so pretty because no one ventures to this corner,

"Who would want to touch this?…who wants the memory of another's life, something they obviously didn't want either." I start to flip through them, still. 
The old baseball trophy's gleam demands my attention 
— his name is still engraved in this one. 
<br>

&nbsp;I'm trying to remind myself that it's just memories...someone else's, that is. But my faintest whisper sees myself in everyone these days. 
Even if the semblance of family is weak in me. I still have enough of a picture through the movies, and shows. Books I've read...childhood friends who have taken me in like a stray cat when all the wandering I do just starts to look a bit pathetic. 
Maybe I am that sports trophy too — with its words, names, phrases, titles…engraved right onto me. 

I wear everyone as if I'm their champion. 
<br>

&nbsp;After several glances all over, scanning them back and forth, hoping to find a clue of some sorts. I put it down for the first time. 
Maybe I thought I would know one of these names, maybe one of them would be famous by now, and returning it to them would give me good karma or something. 
Or, it's just looking for an excuse to finally do a favor for somebody else.

Really, I was hoping one of these things I pick up is my own. 
Something I get to finally take home that doesn't outgrow me in 6 months like cloth, or break down in two like the shitty airfyer I keep justifying the price of. 
As if that's worth the headache of the electrical fire I know it's bound to cause one day.

You can't outgrow your past. 
Especially the one you never knew you forgot in the first place. 
I placed everything back, a little bit nicer than before. 
Now with some cohesion in how it laid, this time...it seemed to actually hold some respect. 
Despite it all, my head was circular in thought. I figured that was my cue to make the pilgrimage home. 
Still, it felt embarrassing to leave a thrift store with nothing in hand — I saw a really pretty skirt on the way in. Sketched floral art, monochromatic patterns, and short enough to get a glance or two…it had definitely caught my attention.
Trudging out with that, I felt a little bit better towards myself on the walk home...
"That'll do."

Repeating all my affirmations, and feedback loops. 
Whilst searching for the keys that shoud be draped around my neck for this exact reason — not to ever forget, thankfully. 

They were still there.
