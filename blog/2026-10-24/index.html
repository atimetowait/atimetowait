There’s these small moments in and out of change—transitionary life droplets that for months and years seem to keep me up later and then, more agitated throughout the day for it …it all feels the same after a while. The saying that you shouldn’t name things you don’t want to get attached to. The cat you found on the side of the road, the dog you’re just supposed to be fostering until someone who can “actually take care of it” comes along. Nicknames and why they stick and after so, so long of being called it. Your given one just feels slightly to the left of you. But maybe the nickname was given as recklessly as your first, you were just there to see the moment this time, and feel the time shift from that point forward. Like a badge of honor or something you keep concealed in the back of your pocket. Many carry a gun, or a knife, there. Maybe their wallet, filled to the brim with cards of monetary value, and more with personal identification on it. Though, none of them have the nickname on them, or the same cadence that it has when someone you love shouts it from across a crowded room. I think a lot of things facing the no return, but I often continue on, through…past it. Into the walking turning to jogs and sooner or later, you’re in a full sprint. So you keep going and running, and not letting pace, not keeping pace to anyone, outrunning your heart rate. Trying so towards the sun. So you do and then the moon takes over and all the rays refracting on the trees get softer—you can stare a bit calmer now. I never know when to mark flags in the sand and declare what is new of the old. When the old is still all around, can you even call anything new? I guess you build up and out from it, the inner circle that collectively grows. Looking around, maybe it’s just the inside of ourselves we see enough of to grow tired of it. Then you remove it all and start anew, I suppose. Working from the ground up in replacing—I’ve gotten half way through so many times only to hate it more than my entry self. After so many times of ‘again’, it starts to feel like busy work. There’s more stuff around that I don’t want to tend to, and I probably won’t today, or tonight. But I’ll wake up tomorrow, probably won’t do it then. Maybe after running and sprinting and outpacing the heart rate for so long—you earn the right to jog backwards a bit, I think so at least. The sun is too hot to chase forever, the moon keeps too many shades hidden. If the sun is to allow me to see, and the moon to allow me to ponder. I want to sit in the middle-part and think about all it is I’ve done until I’ve seen the cycles of the seasons in their present tense, and not have to repeat on what I’ve learned from my past, over and over again.
